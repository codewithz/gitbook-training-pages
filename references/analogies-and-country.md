# Funny analogies and country-based examples

Contents: how to build an analogy, country starting points, what to avoid.

## How to build an analogy

A joke that does not teach is just noise. Build the analogy so the humor and the concept are the same thing.

1. Pick the concept's job in one sentence. For virtual environments: "keeps each project's packages separate".
2. Find a daily experience candidates know, from the country they named, with the same job.
3. Map the parts one to one in a small table, so the analogy holds up under questions.
4. Exaggerate the failure mode. The funniest part is usually what goes wrong when the concept is missing.
5. Return to the technical term in the last sentence, so the analogy hands the candidate back to the real topic.

One strong analogy per concept beats three weak ones. Use a second only when the first breaks down.

Example mapping table:

| In the story | In the system |
|---|---|
| The shared fridge in a flat | The global Python install |
| A flatmate swaps milk for oat milk | One project upgrades a package |
| Everyone's chai tastes wrong | Every other project breaks |
| A personal mini-fridge | A virtual environment |

## Country starting points

These are seeds for everyday touchpoints. Confirm they fit the group, and swap in anything the candidates mention themselves.

| Country | Money and payments | Daily life and transport | Food and small frustrations |
|---|---|---|---|
| India | Rupee, UPI QR codes | Mumbai local trains, auto-rickshaw bargaining | Chai breaks, monsoon traffic, tatkal ticket rush |
| Sri Lanka | Rupee, bank QR payments | Tuk-tuks, Colombo traffic | Kottu, tea, rain on the way to the office |
| Nepal | Rupee, mobile wallets | Micro-buses, hill roads | Momo, dal bhat, slow Kathmandu mornings |
| Saudi Arabia | Riyal, mada cards | Riyadh traffic, ride-hailing | Karak, dates and coffee, midday heat |
| UAE | Dirham, tap cards | Metro, Salik toll gates | Shawarma, brunch culture, summer air conditioning |
| Oman | Omani rial | Muscat roads, buses | Halwa, shuwa, long coastal drives |
| Malaysia | Ringgit, e-wallets | KL traffic, ride-hailing | Teh tarik at the mamak stall, durian debates |
| Singapore | Dollar, PayNow | MRT, queues | Hawker centre ordering, kopi codes |
| Brunei | Dollar | Gadong, water taxis | Ambuyat, quiet evenings |

Use these for story settings, currency in datasets and names of places. Datasets use the local currency and realistic amounts for that country.

## Matching the industry

When the candidates work in a known industry, such as banking, telecom or retail, pull the analogy and the dataset from it: a branch queue, a recharge, a checkout counter. The same technical idea then lands twice, once as a joke and once as something they recognize from work.

## What to avoid

- Religion, caste, ethnicity, politics, nationality stereotypes and appearance. Humor works through shared frustrations, not through groups of people.
- Jokes at the candidates' expense. The target of the joke is the situation, the tool or the trainer.
- Naming the client's company, their products or their competitors in a joke.
- Local references the trainer cannot verify. If unsure a reference is current, use a more general one.
- Humor in error and security sections where candidates could lose data or money. There, be clear first, and keep the joke small.

## Mixed groups

If candidates come from several countries, use settings every region shares: a crowded commute, a slow queue, a shared kitchen, a delayed delivery. Add a one-line local variant per region only when the trainer asks for it.
